# LEVEL UP Product Quality Standard

This document is the mandatory quality standard for creating or materially changing LEVEL UP apps.

The goal is not merely to implement requested features. The goal is to finish a product that a first-time user can understand immediately, that creates a real change in the user's situation, feels good to operate, has a reason to be used again, and is worth recommending or sharing.

Treat implementation as a full product-team task: product design, game design, UX research, copywriting, engineering, QA, and final product review.

## 1. Define the exact use moment before coding

Write one concrete sentence covering all of the following:

- who uses it;
- what situation makes them open it;
- what they feel at that moment;
- what must be different when they finish.

Abstract targets such as "raise self-esteem" are not enough.

Prefer a concrete moment such as:

> A person who thinks they may have said something strange in a meeting and keeps replaying it after getting home uses this for three minutes to stop the mental replay.

If this cannot be stated clearly, do not start substantial implementation.

## 2. Choose one central benefit

The user does not want a "training" for its own sake. They want a result such as:

- feel lighter;
- forget or stop replaying something;
- start moving;
- make a decision;
- stop hesitating;
- continue a habit;
- become better at a skill.

Choose one central benefit. Do not blur it by adding unrelated features.

## 3. Increase subject resolution before designing

If the theme touches psychology, behavior science, habits, communication, work methods, learning, or another existing body of knowledge, do not build from vague general knowledge.

When needed, investigate enough to understand:

- why the problem occurs;
- common failure patterns;
- useful approaches;
- what the problem looks like in real life;
- how novice and advanced cases differ.

Do not turn an explanation article into a game by adding buttons.

## 4. Make the interaction itself teach the target behavior

A row of three choices is not automatically a game.

Choose an interaction that matches the thinking or behavior being trained, for example:

- swipe;
- tap;
- sort;
- discard;
- divide into bins;
- drag away;
- collect;
- protect;
- repeatedly judge under a time limit;
- make the visual state change.

Examples:

- If the theme is "letting go", the user should actually move or dismiss the thought.
- If the theme is task separation, the user should sort items into "mine" and "theirs".

The operation itself should carry the learning.

## 5. The first 10 seconds are the highest priority

Assume the user will not read a manual.

Within ten seconds they must understand:

1. what this app is;
2. why it is relevant to them;
3. what they should do next.

Prefer:

> open → touch immediately → understand while doing

Avoid long tutorials and explanatory walls of text.

## 6. Every action must return a meaningful response

Do not make interaction feel like "press next and receive another prompt".

Use only the feedback that helps understanding or satisfaction, such as:

- movement;
- sound;
- vibration;
- state change;
- immediate short feedback;
- progress changes;
- visual transformation.

Effects must strengthen meaning, not decorate weak interaction.

## 7. Content must stay specific and varied

Do not make ten thoughtful questions followed by dozens of near-duplicates.

Use concrete situations across appropriate domains, including:

- work;
- family;
- friends;
- relationships;
- SNS;
- money;
- future concerns;
- failure;
- comparison;
- evaluation;
- small everyday events.

Difficulty should deepen from obvious cases, to ambiguous cases, to realistic cases that require judgment.

## 8. Return "why", not only right or wrong

Keep feedback short and reusable in real life.

Bad:

> Wrong.

Better:

> You can decide how you prepare next time. Whether the other person thinks you are competent is their side.

The user should leave with a rule they can reuse.

## 9. Show genuine growth

For repeatable training, include at least one meaningful sign of improvement, such as:

- level based on actual skill;
- weak area detection;
- before/after comparison;
- accuracy by situation;
- speed with preserved accuracy;
- unlocked difficulty;
- historical judgment comparison.

Do not add points, coins, levels, or gacha that merely count activity without reflecting learning.

## 10. Make the ending prove that the session mattered

Do not end with only "Good job" or "Completed".

Show what changed during this use, for example:

- what the user can now distinguish;
- what thought they let go of;
- what action became small enough to start;
- what score or feeling changed from before to after;
- what judgment pattern was strongest or weakest.

When useful, save this result so the next visit has context.

## 11. Create a real reason to return

Decide whether the app is one-shot or becomes stronger through repetition.

For a repeatable app, return value may come from:

- different problems;
- deeper difficulty;
- weak-area adaptation;
- comparison with yesterday;
- a changing challenge;
- accumulated skill evidence.

Do not add a login bonus with no product meaning.

## 12. Title quality gate

Do not keep the first working title automatically.

### Market/title discovery checklist

When title discovery materially matters, investigate candidate wording using the intended market sources available to the project, including Google and Amazon search suggestions where appropriate. Do not invent research results that were not actually checked.

Choose one main user motive when relevant:

- be liked;
- be right;
- make money;
- save time;
- stand out;
- have fun;
- improve health;
- gain convenience;
- satisfy a desire;
- be praised;
- feel important;
- save money;
- become more attractive;
- feel comfortable;
- become happier;
- gain knowledge;
- satisfy curiosity;
- escape fear;
- escape guilt.

Then check all of the following:

- Does the title contain a clear benefit?
- Does it feel like useful/new information rather than generic wording?
- Does it create curiosity?
- Does it sound quick or easy enough for the actual app?
- Is the promise restrained rather than exaggerated?
- Is it direct?
- Is the target user identifiable?
- Is the main point short and simple?
- Can it be made more concrete?
- Does it logically match the actual app?
- Is it free from meaningless wordplay or cleverness for its own sake?
- If uniqueness matters, has the title been checked against Amazon or other relevant market usage rather than assumed unique?

Meaning is more important than style.

## 13. Review the UI as a first-time user

For every screen, check:

- Is the next action obvious?
- Is the text readable on a phone?
- Is important information visually dominant?
- Are controls easy to tap?
- Is the same information repeated unnecessarily?
- Can explanation be removed because the UI itself communicates the idea?
- Does each screen have one primary purpose?
- Is back/exit/home behavior clear?
- Are mistakes hard to make and easy to recover from?

Do not use explanatory copy to rescue confusing UI.

## 14. Remove generic "AI app" patterns

Treat these as warning signs:

- generic gradient cards with no functional reason;
- decorative emoji grids;
- universal encouragement such as "You can do it";
- repeated obvious three-choice questions;
- abstract questions;
- long explanations;
- copied templates across unrelated apps;
- a level number that rises without real skill change;
- unrelated gacha or rewards.

The experience should make sense only for this app's specific problem.

## 15. Prove why this app cannot be replaced by another LEVEL UP app

Before calling the product complete, answer in one sentence:

> Why can this app not simply be deleted and replaced by an existing LEVEL UP app?

If the answer is weak, merge it into an existing app or redesign the interaction and benefit until the distinction is real.

## 16. Actually use the app from start to finish

Source review is not a playtest.

Test as a user:

- first visit;
- start;
- main interaction;
- wrong choice/failure path;
- correct path;
- back;
- reload;
- end;
- revisit;
- mobile layout.

If at any point the tester thinks "this is tedious", "I do not know what this means", "this is the same thing again", or "nothing happened when I touched it", fix that before completion.

If browser interaction is unavailable, do not invent evidence. Mark the relevant test unverified and do not claim full completion.

## 17. Five-score final gate

Score each area from 0 to 10 based on actual observed behavior, not optimism:

- Clarity — can a first-time user use it without explanation?
- Usefulness — does it create a real change in the user's situation?
- Interaction quality — does operating it feel responsive and satisfying?
- Uniqueness — is there a real reason this app exists separately?
- Repeat value — is there a reason to use it again when appropriate?

Every item must be at least **7/10**.

Do not average away a weak category. If one score is below 7, improve the app and retest.

## 18. Implementation complete is not product complete

The following do not count as completion by themselves:

- code written;
- build passed;
- PR opened;
- main merged.

For user-facing production work, production must be deployed and the actual screen/behavior must be verified according to `HOSTING_POLICY.md` before claiming production completion.

## 19. Product-led discovery is part of product quality

LEVEL UP apps are intended to become their own discovery assets rather than relying mainly on continuous manual promotion.

When it fits the app and does not distort the core experience, prefer:

- a useful standalone page that search/AI systems can understand;
- a result worth sharing because it is meaningful to the user;
- a shared result that naturally lets another person try the same experience;
- a clear title/description based on the problem solved;
- related-app links that continue the user's actual goal;
- anonymous aggregate insights only when they provide genuine value.

Do **not** create thin SEO pages for every wording variation. One strong, useful app page is preferable to many low-value pages.

## Required implementation artifacts for material LEVEL UP work

For a new LEVEL UP app, or a material redesign of one, create or update the following inside `apps/<slug>/` unless the app architecture makes that impossible:

### `SPEC.md`

Must include:

- exact use moment;
- central benefit;
- why the problem occurs / design rationale;
- core interaction and why it matches the skill;
- first-10-second experience;
- success condition;
- uniqueness vs existing LEVEL UP apps;
- repeat-use strategy or explicit one-shot rationale.

### `QUALITY.md`

Must include evidence for:

- first-time clarity;
- mobile readability and tap targets;
- wrong/correct paths;
- reload/revisit behavior;
- real browser playtest status;
- final five scores with each score at least 7 if marked complete;
- any unverified items stated explicitly.

Do not fabricate test evidence.

## Final question

Before completion, ask:

> If I genuinely had this problem, would I open this app again?

If the answer is no, it is not finished.

Prefer one 90-point app over ten 60-point apps.
